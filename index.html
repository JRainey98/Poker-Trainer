♠outs. A LITTLE PRACTICE. A BETTER INSTINCT.

Count your outs. Trust your math.

An out is an unseen card that improves your hand to a higher hand category using at least one hole card. We count pairs, two pair, trips, straights, flushes, full houses, quads, and straight flushes. Overlapping draws count each card once.

Pair, trips, and quads outs must match a hole-card rank. A board pairing on its own doesn’t count as an improvement. Better kickers and higher versions of the same hand category don’t count. An ace can be low in A–2–3–4–5 straights.

Turn ≈ outs × 2%. By the river ≈ outs × 4%. “By the river” includes both chances to hit after the flop. These are practice estimates, capped at 100%. Two aces on a dry, unpaired flop have 2 set outs: turn ≈ 4%, by the river ≈ 8%.

This measures drawing chances, not your chance of beating an opponent. There is no opponent hand, no discount for losing outs, and no backdoor draws requiring two specific cards. Exact figures describe hitting at least one of your currently identified outs; they don’t account for new draws or the board changing later.
